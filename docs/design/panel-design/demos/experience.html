The concept

Motion is how the panel points. The typewriter is how it does it with character. Every move answers a question you have right now. If a move answers nothing, it doesn't happen.

  1. Every move has a job. It explains, directs, confirms, or warns. Decoration alone isn't a job.
  2. Things come from somewhere. What opens grows from what opened it, and goes back there.
  3. The focus ring is the guide. It travels to where you need to look next, so your eyes get carried along.
  4. One loud thing at a time. Your actions are loud. Everything else stays still, so the moving thing is the important thing.
  5. Weight tells consequence. Routine actions feel light. Serious ones feel heavy, before they happen.
  6. Quick to arrive, quiet to leave. You never wait for a landing, and exits never bounce.
  7. One machine, everywhere. Keys strike, the carriage returns, paper feeds, text types, jams judder. The same few actions, so you learn their meaning.

What this means for the document

A new experience chapter comes first, and the motion chapter serves it. Each question becomes something the reviewer checks on every action, on every page:

XP-03 MUST  After an action, focus lands where the next step is.
  Why    The user should never have to hunt for what's next.
  Check  test (focus target) + visual-llm (the ring is visible)
  Broken severity 3
  Do     New page: focus moves to the new page's title field
  Don't  New page: the row appears, focus stays on the button

Reduced motion keeps every answer. The ring jumps rather than slides, and marks and messages still appear.
